12 checks, no AI needed

Glossary term

Flags a phrase that nearly matches a canonical term from the ICAIRE AI Glossary. Abstains when it could mean two terms.
"kernel support machine"→≈ "Kernel Support Vector Machine"

Fake DOI

Resolves every DOI and arXiv id. Only accuses when Crossref, DataCite and doi.org all disown it — a timeout is never an accusation.
doi:10.9999/not-real→unregistered — 3 registries agree

Wrong target

If neither title, authors, nor year appears near the citation, the id may point at a different work.
DOI ≠ the work the prose describes→mismatch flagged

Retracted source

Reads the retraction notice on the cited work. Corrections and errata are not flagged — an amended paper stands.
Wakefield 1998→RETRACTED 2010 — notice linked

Cited, not listed

Every bracketed number in the prose needs an entry in the list, or a reader cannot follow it.
prose cites [5]→the list has no entry [5]

Never cited

The reverse: an entry that no sentence ever cites. Editorial, not an error of fact.
[3] sits in the list→no sentence ever cites it

Duplicate entry

The same number listed twice, or the same work listed under two different numbers.
[6] repeats [1]→same work listed twice

Missing year

A reference with no year of publication. “In press”, “n.d.” and bare URLs are exempt.
an undated entry→flagged ("in press" is fine)

Orphan figure

A figure or table has a caption but no sentence ever points a reader at it.
Figure 1 has a caption→the text never points at it

Ghost figure

The prose points at a figure or table that has no caption anywhere in the document.
"see Figure 2"→no Figure 2 exists

Summary drift

A percentage asserted in the abstract that the body never states. Rounding is fine.
abstract says "45%"→the body never states it

Language gate

Language is detected from the text. A non-English draft is skipped with a reason, never misjudged.
non-English draft→skipped honestly, never misjudged

4 judgements, quote-verified

Does the source say that?

Reads the cited work’s abstract (or full text) and rules whether it supports the claim. Four verdicts, three of them silent.
"their survey achieved 95% accuracy"vsabstract: "we do not report accuracy"→unsupported — both quotes verified verbatim first

Self-contradiction

Deterministic code pairs numbers reported for the same metric; the model rules whether they are really one quantity.
"91.4% accuracy"…"99.2% accuracy"→same metric, two values

Missing baseline

Checks methods and results against a fixed rubric, reporting only gaps a stated claim depends on.
"a 12-point improvement"+no baseline in Methods→gap anchored to that exact claim

Overclaiming

A lexicon finds strong claims; the model weighs whether the surrounding evidence can carry them.
"one trial proves it eliminates hallucination"→one trial cannot carry that claim
